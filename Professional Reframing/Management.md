### 🧠 Persona 1: Management (Middle to Senior)

| Scenario | Comeback | Professional Reframe |
|----------|----------|------------------------|
| Downplays risk severity | "It’s not severe—until it is. And by then, it’s a headline." | "Let’s align on impact criteria—risk isn’t about what has happened, but what can happen." |
| Delays decision on critical security control | "Indecision is a decision—with consequences." | "This needs timely action to avoid downstream exposure. Can we agree on a deadline?" |
| Asks for reports but doesn’t read them | "I can summarize it in one word: avoidable." | "Let me distill this into key points tied to your priorities for faster absorption." |
| Claims security slows business down | "So does rebuilding your reputation from scratch." | "Strong security enables trust—without it, growth is fragile." |
| Uses compliance as an excuse to avoid real security | "Compliance isn’t immunity." | "Regulations are baselines, not blueprints for resilience." |
| Pushes accountability back to you after accepting risk | "Risk accepted doesn’t mean risk forgotten." | "We can formalize the decision, but shared accountability ensures ongoing visibility." |
| Says, 'We’ve never had an incident, why change?' | "Neither did Blockbuster—until they did." | "Lack of incidents may reflect luck, not posture. We can’t bet the future on that." |
| Prioritizes cost-cutting over cyber investment | "Breaches cost more than prevention, every time." | "We can right-size investment by mapping it directly to exposure." |
| Delegates cyber to IT with no C-level visibility | "You don’t delegate fire safety to janitors." | "Cyber needs board-level oversight to reflect its strategic importance." |
| Refuses MFA for their own account | "If attackers saw your access level, they'd target you first." | "Let’s model the right behavior from the top—it’s leadership by example." |
| Forwards phishing emails instead of reporting properly | "Forwarding is how we get *more* phishing, not less." | "Let’s use the report button—it helps SOC learn and defend faster." |
| Wants exceptions for friends or favorite teams | "Cybersecurity doesn’t run on favoritism—it runs on controls." | "Exceptions increase risk and complexity—let’s look at consistent application." |
| Hires contractors with no security clearance | "Unverified access is the front door to insider threat." | "Let’s validate clearance to match our risk appetite and controls." |
| Misunderstands risk register ratings | "Green doesn’t mean gone—it means ‘monitored.’" | "I’m happy to walk through how we define risk levels—it helps with prioritization." |
| Shuts down issues without asking technical teams | "Closing your eyes doesn’t make the fire disappear." | "Let’s validate with engineering—better safe than exposed." |
| Creates duplicate initiatives that conflict with cyber programs | "Fragmented programs lead to fragmented defense." | "Let’s unify efforts so we avoid redundant spend and misaligned outcomes." |
| Says, “That’s not a board issue.” | "Until the headlines say otherwise." | "It’s board-relevant if it impacts trust, continuity, or financials—which this does." |
| Overrules Conditional Access enforcement | "Security isn't a buffet—you don't just skip what you don't like." | "Overrides should be risk-assessed and documented to ensure alignment." |
| Asks to whitelist legacy apps but won’t decommission them | "We’re not in the business of keeping liabilities alive." | "We’ll need a roadmap to migrate safely—or isolate and monitor aggressively." |
| Responds emotionally to factual threat reporting | "Facts don’t care how we feel—they just exploit us." | "Let’s use data-driven dialogue—it keeps us grounded when stakes are high." |
